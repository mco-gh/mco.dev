Three years ago, in the weeks after October 7, 2023, I lost nearly my entire family. I use that phrase carefully, because many people lost their families in the literal sense that day in Israel and in the months that followed in Gaza. Mine are all still alive, but I no longer have any contact with them. They think I've rejected them. I see things a bit differently. Let me explain.

<!--more-->

I've written extensively about <a href="https://mco.dev/is-anti-zionism-antisemitic/" target="_blank">Zionism</a>, <a href="https://mco.dev/is-israel-an-apartheid-state/" target="_blank">apartheid</a>, <a href="https://mco.dev/theres-a-word-for-whats-happening-in-gaza/" target="_blank">genocide</a>, and many <a href="https://mco.dev/tags/palestine/" target="_blank">related topics</a>. In those views, I'm an outlier. I was raised in a culture of <a href="https://mco.dev/i-call-bullshit/" target="_blank">indoctrination</a>. Three times a week I was carted off to Hebrew School, where I was fed a steady diet of dogma featuring, above all else, the sanctity of the state of Israel. I rejected most of it. Nearly everyone around me took it to heart.

Despite those philosophical differences, I maintained mostly happy and supportive relations with my extended family for over 60 years. But all that changed after October 7. Watching the <a href="https://mco.dev/gaza-by-the-numbers/" target="_blank">obliteration of Gaza</a>, the blatant <a href="https://mco.dev/movie-review-naza/" target="_blank">targeting of innocent civilians</a>, and the indifference to Palestinians' <a href="https://mco.dev/free-the-hostages/" target="_blank">basic rights</a>, I realized silence was no longer an option for me.

People like to ask what they would have done as a German citizen while Jews were being sent to concentration camps. I'm not equating the two; the scale and the history are vastly different. But the question it puts to the bystander is the same. When people are confined, stripped of their rights, and killed in enormous numbers by a state that claims to act in your name, do you speak up or do you keep the peace?

So I chose to speak up, loudly and frequently, mostly by email to a large family thread. Let me be the first to admit, I was over the top. I vented my spleen at a vulnerable and sensitive time for others. When people asked to be taken off the thread, I said I would, and then I didn't, and then I started new threads. I regret the stridency of my messaging during that period of raw nerves, and I've apologized multiple times, both for my lack of sensitivity and for my behavior. As I wrote back then:

> I do apologize for saying I would take people off the thread and not doing so and also for starting new threads. I should have just stopped emailing because I antagonized you all, and it was dishonest to say I would do something and then not do it.

So I wasn't innocent. Nevertheless, this got me cut off, and I think it was due to more than my barrage of emails. I think they disliked the message at least as much as the delivery. They stopped talking with me, they declined to see me when I offered to visit, and they repeatedly accused me of having dementia, comparing me to our mother, who tragically lost her grasp of reality in her final years.

Calling this a life disruption would be an understatement. Within a matter of weeks I was disconnected from the people I'd been closest to for my entire life. Siblings, in-laws, nieces, nephews -- all gone. I experienced a kind of grief, and for a long time the absence felt disorienting and surreal. The grief hasn't vanished entirely, but over the past three years it has settled into something quieter and more manageable. I've adjusted to a world with a smaller inner circle -- a core group of people who share my values and, more importantly, <a href="https://mco.dev/take-me-off-this-thread/" target="_blank">accept me for who I am</a>.

To their credit, my brother and sister have reached out and asked me if I'd be open to a reconciliation. Unfortunately, it came with a condition: "the topic" stays off the table. They seem to think that if only I wouldn't talk about it, everything would be fine again.

I understand why they ask. Given how I behaved, wanting a break from my emails is reasonable. We have diametrically opposed perspectives, despite our common upbringing, and plenty of families get along by agreeing not to discuss politics. I also know they're not trying to silence me in the world at large; I'm free to write whatever I like here. But here's what they're missing: I found part of myself after October 7, and I'll never be the same again. This isn't politics to me, and it isn't something I can set aside for the length of a visit.

Asking me not to talk about it is like a family in the American South in the 1960s saying, "We can be close as long as you don't bring up civil rights." It makes the relationship conditional. It requires me to keep a core part of my conscience hidden just to keep the peace.

They think I dropped them. But when the price of belonging is leaving my conscience at the door, I'd say they dropped me.
