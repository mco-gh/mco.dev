    About Bookshelf

    Bookshelf turns a photo of your bookshelf into an interactive page. An AI model finds every book spine in the photo, reads it, and draws a hotspot over it.

    What you can do

    • Browse. Hover over a spine, or tab to it, to see the title and author.
    • Look closer. Select a spine for a short description, the cover, and links to find the book at Open Library, Google Books, a nearby library or Bookshop.org.
    • Search. Type in the search box to light up matching books on the shelf.
    • Correct. If a title is wrong, open the book and choose “Fix this entry” to rename it or remove its outline.
    • Keep and share. Download the shelf as a single web page, or as a zip to host or edit yourself.

    How to use it

    1. Add your Anthropic API key under “Your API key”. Scans are billed to your Anthropic account, roughly $0.40–$0.75 each.
    2. Take a photo of one bookcase or a few shelves, facing the spines.
    3. Choose the photo under “Scan a bookshelf” and select “Scan bookshelf”. A scan takes a minute or two.
    4. Explore the result, fix anything that is off, and give the shelf a name.
    5. Download it. To change a downloaded shelf later, bring it back with “Open a saved shelf”.

    Tips for a good scan

    • Good light and a sharp photo matter most: if you can read a spine in the photo, the scan usually can too.
    • Leaning books are fine. Books cut off by the edge of the photo are skipped unless they can still be identified.
    • For a large bookcase, several closer photos work better than one distant one.

    Your photos and data

    • Bookshelf has no server of its own. Your photo goes straight from your browser to Anthropic’s Claude for analysis, using your API key.
    • Your API key is stored in this browser only: until you close the tab, or on this device if you tick “Remember”. It is sent only to Anthropic.
    • Shelves are saved only in this browser. Clearing the browser’s site data removes them, so download the ones you want to keep.
    • Opening a book looks it up at Open Library, which sends that book’s title and author there.

    Titles are read by AI and can be wrong, especially on worn, thin or partly hidden spines.
